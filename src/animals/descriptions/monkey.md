# Monkey

## Appearance
A primate with a flexible torso, mobile shoulders, grasping hands and feet, and a face that is relatively exposed compared with the body. Body size, limb proportions, muzzle shape, and tail length vary widely by species.

## Body and Surface
Most monkeys have fur over the torso and limbs, with colors ranging from brown and gray to black, gold, or reddish tones. Some species have contrasting patches or facial markings. Fur is generally short to medium length and follows the body contours.

## Head and Features
The face may have exposed skin around the eyes, nose, and mouth. Eyes are forward-facing, and the muzzle may be short or protruding depending on the species. The ears are usually small and rounded; facial skin can differ in color from the coat.

## Limbs, Hands, and Tail
The arms and legs are flexible, with hands and feet adapted for grasping. Most digits have nails, though some species have specialized grooming claws. Tails may be long and used for balance; in some New World monkeys, the tail can be prehensile.

## Modeling and Materials
Identify the species before setting limb lengths, facial shape, and tail function. Use short fur over the body and a distinct skin material for exposed facial areas, palms, and soles. Model hands and feet as grasping structures rather than simple paws.
